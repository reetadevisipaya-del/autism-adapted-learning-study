# Research basis for the interface manipulation

## Autism-adapted condition

- Consistent page structure, navigation, control styling and placement.
- One primary task at a time with clear, visible progress.
- Plain, literal instructions and short content sections.
- Low-arousal colour palette, flat backgrounds and restrained decoration.
- Generous spacing, readable sans-serif body type and clear hierarchy.
- No countdown, no automatic animation and reduced-motion support.
- Optional rather than automatic sound.
- Immediate, neutral acknowledgement of every answer; errors never block progress.
- Semantic structure, keyboard access, visible focus and a skip link.

## Standard comparison condition

The learning material, question order, answers, images, feedback wording and scoring stay identical. Only visual presentation changes. The standard condition adds a high-saturation palette, patterned background, mixed visual emphasis, decorative shadows, denser borders and less consistent visual hierarchy. It remains operable and does not introduce flashing, forced audio or inaccessible controls.

## Methodological cautions

- “Autism-adapted” is not universal: autistic people have diverse and sometimes conflicting sensory and interaction preferences. The condition is a testable bundle of literature-informed features, not a claim that one design suits everyone.
- A formal protocol should define primary and secondary outcomes in advance, randomise or counterbalance condition assignment, document inclusion criteria, and obtain ethics approval and accessible consent.
- Do not call the intentionally busy comparison condition “the total opposite” in participant-facing material; that wording could bias expectations.
- The generated facial-expression photographs are prototype stimuli. Replace them with appropriately licensed and validated stimuli for a formal experiment.
